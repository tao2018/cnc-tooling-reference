Cross-Hole Burr Control Before Final CNC Bore Finishing

When a radial or angled hole breaks into a precision bore, the burr plan must be decided before the bore is finished—not after inspection finds a scratch or folded lip.

Start from the intersection, not the drill size

A cross-hole burr is not just a housekeeping issue. In hydraulic sleeves, valve bodies, sensor bosses and pump covers, a folded edge can scratch the mating part, trap chips, cut an O-ring or change flow. The drawing may call out only a hole size and bore finish, but the manufacturing plan has to decide which tool creates, controls and verifies the intersection edge.

The safest sequence is usually to leave a measurable bore allowance, drill or interpolate the cross feature, remove the internal burr with a programmed tool, then finish the bore after the risky intersection work is complete.

Practical sequencing rules

Leave bore stock until the burr event is over

If the bore is finished first, drilling the cross hole can push a burr into the finished surface and force handwork. Leaving a small, planned allowance lets the final boring tool restore size, roundness and finish after intersection control.

Do not make hand deburring the only plan

Manual scraping may work on an open edge, but it is weak for hidden bore intersections. A programmed back-chamfer, lollipop cutter, orbital brush or controlled reverse toolpath is easier to inspect and repeat.

Match tool edge to material failure mode

Nickel alloys need heat and work-hardening control. Aluminum needs chip evacuation and built-up edge prevention. Polymers need sharp polished tools. Cast iron needs dust control. The same cross-hole drawing can require very different tooling because the burr forms differently.
