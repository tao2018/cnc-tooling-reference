PCD Aluminum Finishing: Inspect Function, Not Mirror Shine

A bright aluminum surface can still fail as a sealing land. Long-wave flatness error, smeared material and a small step where toolpaths join may all hide behind a mirror-like appearance. PCD is useful because it holds a sharp, abrasion-resistant edge, but the tool must be selected around the surface function.

Match the PCD tool to the feature

FeaturePCD decisionRelease check
Broad sealing landBalanced PCD face mill with a controlled wiper edgeFlatness, waviness, Ra and contact pattern
High-silicon aluminum borePositive-rake PCD boring or reaming edgeDiameter, roundness and finish trend
Interrupted casting skinRemove the impact-prone skin with a sacrificial carbide tool firstInspect the PCD edge for microchips before finishing
Ferrous insert boundaryStop the PCD path before steel and use a separate toolCheck the material transition and burr

Protect the finished datum

Measure runout at the cutting edge, not only at the holder. Keep the final pass continuous, prevent chips from crossing the completed land, and avoid hand blending a functional seal. When impact risk is high, separate stock cleanup from PCD finishing.

Use evidence to end tool life

Track edge notching, microchipping, aluminum adhesion and the trend of flatness and surface texture. PCD is not automatically the best tool for every aluminum job: volume, silicon content, abrasion and required surface function must justify it. Conventional ferrous cutting normally calls for coated carbide or CBN instead.
