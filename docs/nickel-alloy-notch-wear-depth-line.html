Why Nickel-Alloy End Mills Notch at One Depth Line

A narrow wear band at the axial depth-of-cut line is not a signal to lower every cutting parameter. It means one small section of the edge repeatedly crosses a boundary between the engaged cut, a work-hardened surface and changing thermal load.

Three checks before changing the tool

Axial contact lineFixed-depth notch wear versus general flank wearConfirm actual wall engagement and corner entry before changing coating.

Coolant and chip exitEdge overheating from chip recuttingVerify the active flute receives coolant and chips leave the next engagement.

Tool-selection consequence

For low radial engagement, a four- or five-flute variable-helix micrograin carbide radius end mill can work, provided chip space, edge hone and a heat-resistant PVD coating match the operation. A dense multi-flute tool is a poor choice for full-slot contact. A worn rougher should not make the final sealing-wall pass.

The practical decision path is documented in the nickel-alloy CNC tooling hub. Compare a deep-hole case in the Inconel 600 thermowell tooling plan with thin-section behavior in the Inconel 718 seal-ring case.

Release rule

Retire the tool when notch depth, load trend, size correction and surface texture move together. Do not wait for unpredictable chipping, and do not hide a deflecting wall or damaged edge with a larger offset.
