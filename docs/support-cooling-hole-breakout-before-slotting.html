Support Cooling-Hole Breakout Before Cutting the Adjacent Slot

When a small cooling hole exits beside a feather-seal slot or thin window, the drawing creates a sequencing problem. If the slot is opened first, the drill breaks through into an unsupported edge. If the hole is drilled first, the remaining material can act as a temporary backing wall—but only when the later slot operation will remove the controlled breakout zone. The correct order comes from the final intersection, not from a generic “holes before milling” rule.

Map the final intersection before choosing the drill

Overlay the hole axis, slot wall and minimum edge distance. Record the effective drill depth before breakthrough and the thickness of the final ligament. In brittle intermetallics, ceramics and heat-resistant alloys, that final ligament often determines whether the exit shears cleanly, forms a burr, or breaks away as a chip. A diameter callout alone cannot select the tool.

Use temporary material as process support

Keep the adjacent slot closed while drilling whenever its stock can support the hole exit without trapping chips. A short micrograin carbide drill with reinforced point geometry, minimum projection and controlled coolant flow reduces bending. Prepare an oblique entry with a small flat mill or a compatible spot angle; a deep mismatched spotting cone can steer the drill.

Reduce feed before the last ligament fails

Breakthrough load should be planned from the actual exit geometry. Reduce feed before the drill reaches the final ligament; do not wait for a spindle-load spike that may arrive after the edge has fractured. Pecking is useful only if it clears chips. Repeated re-entry can rub a heat-resistant alloy or push fragments against the unsupported exit.

Remove the support with a low-force slot operation

After drilling, inspect the entrance and any accessible exit evidence. Then open the slot using a toolpath that avoids pulling the thin wall toward the cutter. A fine-tooth carbide slitting cutter balances side load on a straight narrow slot. A small trochoidal end mill may be better when the slot curves, provided its radial engagement and holder runout are controlled. Leave a separate wall-finishing allowance rather than forcing one tool to rough, release and finish the fragile edge.

Inspect the intersection after both operations

The final check occurs after the slot exposes the hole. Measure edge loss, hole diameter and slot-wall position together. A clean-looking slot is not enough if the drill exit has receded into the functional wall. Establish a stop rule based on growing exit chips, directional burrs, thrust trend or diameter drift; replacing the drill before fracture is cheaper than trying to blend an uncontrolled breakout.

Drawing-based comparisons

  • 96% alumina nozzle plate with micro-orifices and inlet pockets

The process principle is simple: preserve support until the hole has crossed its most fragile boundary, then remove that support with the lowest-force operation that produces the final slot.
